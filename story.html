The Story

From the valley to the mountain

I never planned to drive a Lamborghini full of teenagers around Oregon talking about Jesus. But every hard mile of my own road was preparing me for it.

My name is Bobby Samai. I host Valley to the Mountain — but long before there was a show, there was just a kid in the valley, trying to find his way up the mountain.

I want to tell you where I’ve been, because the young men I drive with need to know I’m not talking down to them from the summit. I’ve been at the bottom. I know what the pit smells like. And I know the name of the One who reached in and pulled me out.

A life God kept rewriting

The early years

A childhood in the valley

A jail cell, and a turning point

In 2006 I spent time in jail in Sacramento County. It was one of the lowest points of my life — and looking back, it was also where the walls got quiet enough for me to start asking bigger questions about who I was becoming. Bobby: adjust wording as you’re comfortable

A few years later

The hardest decision, and a son named Parker

I went through a season of heartbreak when a relationship fell apart early in a pregnancy. There was real pressure to end it. Through prayer — and by God’s grace alone — my son Parker was born. He’s 7 years old now, and he is living proof to me that no situation is too far gone for God to redeem. Bobby: please review this paragraph carefully before publishing

Marriage

Kristi, and a home built to last

This past April, my wife Kristi and I celebrated six years of marriage. After a childhood with no blueprint for a stable home, God gave me the chance to build one — and to become the kind of husband and father I needed when I was young.

2020 · After Covid

Laid off, and lost in the dark

When Covid hit, I lost my career. I was laid off, depressed, and genuinely hopeless. I had no idea how I’d provide for my family. With nowhere else to turn, I turned to Jesus — not as a last resort, but as the only foundation that wouldn’t move under me.

The climb

From rock bottom to a successful exit

Out of that valley, God opened a door into e-commerce. I went to work, learned everything I could, and built multiple six-figure businesses and an eight-figure business — eventually walking away through a successful exit. The provision was real. But the lesson was bigger than money: He is faithful in the valley and on the mountain.

Today

The mountain isn’t the finish line — it’s the platform

I could coast. Instead, I keep thinking about the kid I used to be, and all the young men out there right now with no one in their corner. That’s why the Huracán, the show, and the non-profit exist. The blessing was never meant to stop with me.

So… why a Lamborghini?

Because it gets the door open

I’ll be honest: a supercar is an attention magnet. And for a 13-year-old who’s shut down every adult who ever tried to “mentor” him, that magnet matters. The Huracán is the reason he says yes to the ride.

Once we’re moving, the car disappears and the conversation is all that’s left. No phones buzzing, no audience, nowhere to run. Just two people, a long stretch of Oregon highway, and the freedom to be honest. Some of the most important conversations of a young man’s life can happen in the passenger seat.

Every dollar the show earns goes back into the mission — the car payment first, and then the non-profit that will outlast the show.

“He brought me up out of a desolate pit, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure.”

— Psalm 40:2

If you’re a young man in the valley — or a parent of one — there’s a seat in this car with your name on it.
