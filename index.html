The mission

Every young man deserves someone who believes in him

Too many boys in the Willamette Valley are walking a hard road without a guide — no father at the table, no mentor in their corner, no one who has been through the valley and come out the other side. Valley to the Mountain exists to change that, one honest conversation at a time.

The Huracán

A door-opener, not the point

A 2017 Lamborghini Huracán turns heads — and that is exactly why it works. For a young man who would never sit down for a “talk,” the chance to ride shotgun in a supercar is the invitation that gets him in the seat.

But the car is just the bridge. Once the doors close and the road opens up, it becomes the one place a kid can be honest without an audience — no classroom, no pressure, no one watching but the road ahead.

How a ride works

Three simple steps

1

A guardian applies

A parent or guardian fills out the application and signs the consent and media-release forms. Nothing happens without their full knowledge and approval.

2

We go for a drive

Bobby picks the young man up for a 1–2 hour drive through the valley. We talk about whatever is real — family, struggles, plans, faith, and the road ahead.

3

The story gets told

With privacy protections and editing in place, the best of that conversation becomes an episode that encourages thousands of other kids walking the same road.

Safety & trust

Built for parents to say yes with confidence

  • Guardian consent first. A parent or guardian completes and signs every form before any ride.
  • Privacy protected. Sensitive details and identities are protected; guardians approve what is shared.
  • Background-checked host. Bobby is committed to background checks and transparent, accountable practices.
  • Off-camera when it matters. The hardest conversations stay between us; nothing private is ever published.

Why I do this

I’ve walked the valley myself

A homeless childhood. A jail cell in 2006. A career wiped out after Covid, and a season of real hopelessness. I know what the bottom feels like — and I know the One who reached down and pulled me out of it. That’s the road I want to walk with these young men.

The bigger vision

From episodes to trade-school scholarships

This is a 12-month commitment with a long-term heart. Funds the show earns first cover the Huracán, then go toward building a non-profit that helps fund trade-school programs for the young men who choose that path after high school — turning conversations into careers.
